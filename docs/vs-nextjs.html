The Core Problems with Next.js for AI Builders

1. Cross-Language Bridge Overhead

If your application leverages Python data science libraries (Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn) or proprietary AI pipelines, building your web layer in Next.js forces you to maintain two separate runtimes: a Node.js web server and a Python worker process. You spend hours writing inter-process communication, serializing JSON payloads, and debugging distributed network failures across containers.

With FastAPI, your web endpoints, database sessions, and machine learning models live in the same unified runtime. You can call your AI pipelines directly within async endpoint handlers with zero network serialization overhead.

2. The Tax Compliance Trap (Direct Stripe vs Polar MoR)

Most Next.js boilerplates wire up raw Stripe Checkout. What they do not tell you is that when you sell digital software globally, you are legally responsible for calculating and remitting Value Added Tax (VAT) across 27 EU member states, Goods and Services Tax (GST) in Australia and the UK, and sales tax across US states.

PyLaunchpad solves this through Polar.sh as a Merchant of Record. Polar handles all international sales tax compliance on their platform, remitting local taxes while depositing cleared funds directly into your bank account.

When Should You Still Use Next.js?

Next.js is the superior choice if you are building an intricate client-side single page application with complex drag-and-drop user interfaces, rich multi-user canvas collaboration (like Figma), or extensive mobile web interactions.

However, if your value proposition is an AI API, a data processing tool, an automation utility, or a developer-focused micro-SaaS, FastAPI delivers higher throughput, lower operational costs, and faster time to first sale.
